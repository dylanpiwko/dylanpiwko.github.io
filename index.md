<h2 id="about">About</h2>

I am a first-year aerospace engineering student at the University of Michigan focused on GNC and unmanned systems. I fly high-powered rockets and co-authored a NASA Technical Memorandum during my internship at NASA Ames.

My current project is Kestrel, a flight computer for a 4-inch rocket designed to reach Mach 1.7. That is beyond what many hobby altimeters handle well, so I designed the sensors, power, and firmware to measure supersonic flight and locate the rocket after landing. I designed a four-layer board in KiCad with GNSS tracking, LoRa telemetry, and four pyro channels. I've flown high-power rockets since 2023, including two-stage and clustered-motor builds, and hold a National Association of Rocketry Level 1 certification.

At NASA Ames in summer 2025, I was one of six interns who designed Aeropulse: a network of autonomous drones and stair-capable robots for moving organs and medicine between urban hospitals without manual handoffs. I examined routing constraints, urban airspace integration, and system-level trade-offs, ultimately presenting the work to more than 50 NASA staff members. Our model projected deliveries 47–59% faster than current methods. This project strengthened my interest in advanced air mobility and autonomous systems.

This fall, I joined a team of Embry-Riddle students in the EUROCONTROL PRC Data Challenge, building CatBoost and LightGBM machine learning models to predict taxi-out times at 10 major European airports. I lowered our team's official RMSE from 276.53 to 267.34 seconds. In January, I'm excited to start work with Firefly Aerospace on the catch recovery system for the Eclipse launch vehicle as part of the Perot Jain TechLab.

I have worked mainly in SolidWorks, KiCad, Python, Microsoft Office, OpenRocket, and RockSim.

I am looking for summer 2027 internships in GNC, unmanned systems, or systems engineering.
